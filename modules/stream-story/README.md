# Announcement story

You are about to stream, release a video or go live, and you want to say so on Instagram? This module makes **a short animated video** that announces the event of your choice: its title, the day and time, a countdown ("In 2 h 15", "Tonight at 7:30 PM", "Tomorrow"), the game and its cover, your logo, the people who join you, and even a sentence of your own. You watch it move before making it, then you **download the file** and post it as a story.

Nothing to install on your computer, nothing to set up to begin: everything happens in the module's page.

## Getting started
1. **Install the module.** If you have a schedule ("Online calendar" or "Hand-written streams", in the catalogue), your next streams show up on their own. If you also have the site's YouTube channel or Twitch channel, your scheduled videos and lives and your Twitch schedule join the same list. With none of this, the module tells you what to install, and you can choose **"No event"** and write the information yourself for the video.
2. **Open the module's page** ("Announcement story" in the menu, or in Extensions).
3. **It is already ready**: when it opens, **the next event** (among all your sources, sorted by date and time) is already chosen and the preview shows it. To publish it as it is, click **"Generate now"**. To adjust it, go through three steps, in this order:
   1. **Event**: keep the next event, pick another one in the list ("Change event"), or click **"No event, I write it myself"** (the information then starts empty).
   2. **Information**: every thing the video can show is one item, filled in from the event or empty: title, day and time, game (its cover is found by itself), countdown, your sentence, guest streamers, logo and channel name. Correct any of them, and **remove one with its "In the video" switch** (the same tick as before, now next to the field: switch it off and the item leaves the preview; switch it on and it comes back). The language of the dates is under "More options".
   3. **Style**: ten cards with a live thumbnail, then the format, the "Made with Curiosa" mention, and under "More options" the length and the safe zones. Models live here too.
4. **The preview is always on screen.** On steps 1 and 2 it shows your video in a **plain default style ("Minimal / text only")**, resting on its finished image so you can read it (press "Play" to see it move): you edit the content first and choose the look last. On step 3 it switches to the chosen style, and clicking a card updates it at once. **Click "Generate the video"** (step 3, the last one): the button shows the progress ("Making the video… 42 %"), and "Cancel" stops everything whenever you want. The file downloads: it is named for example `annonce-2026-10-22-vertical.mp4`. The buttons stay visible, even on a phone.
5. **"Generate now"** (steps 1 and 2) skips everything else: it makes the video **in the style the preview shows**, and its name is on the button ("Generate now, Minimal / text only style"). That style then becomes the chosen one (step 3 shows its card selected), so a video is never made in a style you have not seen. If you already chose a style (or loaded a model), that style is the one shown and used. If a day you wrote is not valid, the field says so and nothing starts.
6. **A model or your AI assistant's preparation** fills everything in, style included: the editor then opens directly on step 3 (Style), where the chosen style is already applied; steps 1 and 2 are one click back to check the information.

## What you choose, for each video
- **The event**: the next upcoming one by default (never an event that has already started while one is still to come), or another one in the short "Change event" list (see below), or **no event** (the information fields start empty). Changing the event refills the title, day, time and game from the new event and touches nothing else: style, sentence and guest streamers stay.
- **What is shown** (step 2, one "In the video" switch per item): the title, the day and time, the countdown, the game and its cover (when known), the logo and name of your channel, your sentence, the guest streamers. **The countdown is unticked by default** (since 2.7.0): tick it to add a line such as "Tonight at 7:30 PM". Models you saved keep what they stored. Picking the "Animated countdown" style ticks it for you, with a short note under the style list, and unticks it again if you change style without touching it.
- **Your sentence**: 140 characters at most, 3 lines at most. It is written as you type it.
- **The style** (the content is the same, only the animation changes). Each card in the picker shows a live thumbnail of the real animation, with your own event, colours, logo and cover:
  - **Neon / glitch**: glowing text that flickers and glitches, on a dark background.
  - **Sliding poster**: the cover and the title slide in, like a movie poster.
  - **Animated countdown**: big numbers roll toward the time of the stream (needs the countdown element).
  - **Minimal / text only**: a plain background, the text appears letter by letter.
  - **TV channel** (`broadcast`): a strip of flat colour bars wipes in, the title and the time arrive as lower-thirds with crisp masks, and a thin ticker at the bottom carries the day and the countdown. Calm, flush left, one grotesque sans.
  - **Kinetic type** (`kinetic`): the text is the picture. A flat field of your colour, tight capitals slamming up through rectangular masks, every other line cut in a little, the time as big as the width allows with an outlined echo of it cropped by its block, and one accent bar.
  - **Film title card** (`cinema`): letterbox bars close in, a serif title fades up slowly, the details are set in tracked capitals and the guests roll as end credits. A slow push-in, a hair of gate weave and film grain.
  - **Arcade 8-bit** (`arcade`): everything on an 8 px grid and moving in steps (10 a second), monospaced capitals with a chunky shadow, bevelled panels with notched corners that slide in from alternating sides, covers and photos that arrive as a mosaic that sharpens in four steps.
  - **Paper collage** (`collage`): a paper table, strips of cut and torn paper with tape, a print with a white border for the cover, the time as a rough ink stamp. Everything sits a little crooked and moves like stop motion (8 steps a second), calming down at the end.
  - **Geometric poster** (`geometric`): flat shapes (a circle, a quarter circle, a bar) built from your colours on a strict grid, big numerals set flush left; every shape and every line lands exactly on a beat.
- **The format**:
  - **Vertical (9:16)**: 1080 × 1920, the format of a story.
  - **Passport (4:5)**: 1080 × 1350, Instagram's almost-square portrait.
- **The duration** (step 3, under "More options"): from 5 to 15 seconds (15 by default, the length of a story). Every style scales its timings to the duration: the composition is complete and still for the last fifth of the video.
- **The language of the words written in the video** (step 2, under "More options"): French or English (the days, the countdown, "WITH", "UP NEXT"…).
- **The small "Made with Curiosa" mention** (checkbox, **unticked by default**: nothing is added without your choice): a discreet small text, centred at the bottom of the video, on a semi-transparent pill in the colours of the background, readable on light and dark backgrounds. It is placed **just above the area that Instagram's interface covers** (account name, reply bar), to stay visible, and the rest of the video is laid out above it. It never moves, present from the first to the last image, also in the preview. The word "Curiosa" follows the logo's style (Fraunces italic, as in the site's footer). It stays in English whatever the language of the video (it is a brand name). No link, nothing is sent anywhere. **Your choice is remembered** for your next stories; a model does not keep it.

The video is **silent**, which suits Instagram very well. The colours and the logo are those of your site.

## Changing the event: the list
A single list gathers **everything that is dated and upcoming**, from all the sources you installed, **sorted by date and time**:

| Source | What it brings | What it needs |
|---|---|---|
| **Planning** | your streams (those of "Online calendar" and "Hand-written streams") | an installed schedule |
| **YouTube** | your scheduled **public lives and premieres** | the site's YouTube channel, with its channel id, and the site's YouTube key (free, entered once for the whole site) |
| **Twitch** | the **schedule published on your Twitch channel** (cancelled appointments and vacations are ignored) | the site's Twitch channel with the Twitch application set up |

No source is mandatory, and there is **nothing to set up in Story**: an installed source appears in the list by itself. A module that publishes other dated events can join in the same way (see "For developers").

- **Each row** shows a pill with the source, the type (stream, live, premiere, video…), the day and time **in your site's time zone** (or the one of the "Time zone" setting, see below) and the title. When the event has its own time zone and the time is not the same, the local time is added in brackets.
- **One click** chooses the event: the video fills itself in (title, day, time, countdown, game and cover when known) and the list closes.
- **Search**: the search and the source buttons only appear when there are many events (more than 8); type a word of the title, the game or a source. **Filter**: the buttons keep a single source (with the number of events of each).
- **Short list**: 5 events first, then "Show more events" adds 10. The list stops at the **next 200** events, all sources together. An event that has already started (less than 3 h ago) stays in the list, marked "Already started"; two events at the same moment are pointed out under the chosen event.
- **No duplicates**: a stream announced by both your schedule and your Twitch schedule (same title, start within 15 minutes) appears **only once**, with the mention "Also in: Twitch". Two events with different titles stay two rows.
- **If a source does not answer**, the list says so politely **below it** ("YouTube is not answering right now") and shows the other sources; if it answered less than 15 minutes ago, its latest dates are kept and the sentence warns that they may have changed. A "Try again" button appears. It is **never** a blocking error, and you can always **write the event yourself**.
- **Source to set up**: the line under the list says what to do in plain words (for example "To see your scheduled videos and lives, add your YouTube key in Settings, on the site's YouTube connector page").
- **What YouTube does not show**: with the site's key, YouTube only gives **public lives and premieres**. A scheduled **private** (or unlisted) video **cannot be seen**: no key allows it, you would have to sign in to your YouTube account, which the site does not ask for. The page says so under the list; for these videos, write the event yourself.
- **Writing the event yourself**: the button under the list opens a title, a day and an optional time. The "Back to the event list" button closes it. If there is no event at all, this form opens by itself.

The event you choose is **remembered in the story request** (its id, given by its source and stable, and the source). When your assistant prepares the video, it is found again when the link is opened, even if it is far down the list; if it no longer exists (past, moved, or silent source), the page says so and takes the next event.

## The safe zones
Instagram hides part of the image: at the top and the bottom of a story (the account name, the reply bar), and on the sides of a portrait when it is shown in the profile grid. The module keeps **everything that matters in the middle**, inside safe margins (vertical: about 250 pixels at the top, 340 at the bottom and 64 on each side; passport: 60 at the top and the bottom, 64 on each side).

To check, tick **"Show the safe zones"** (step 3, under "More options"): they appear hatched on the preview, with their explanation. They are **never** drawn in the video. Decoration that bleeds off the edge on purpose (the outlined echo of the time in "Kinetic type", the shapes of "Geometric poster", the paper strips) is never essential: the words always stay inside.

## Your guest streamers

- **The people of the schedule**: when the chosen stream lists some ("With …"), they are added to the video with their name and photo (initials without a photo), after the ones you choose; beyond 6, a "+N" pill. Only displayable Contacts cards are shown (same rule as the Planning: neither "to review", nor "Do not show"). A box "Add the people listed with this stream" (ticked at the start, folded under "Guest streamers") removes them.
They come from **Contacts**: Story keeps neither their name, nor their handles, nor their photo, only the card number (so nothing to retype, and a name corrected in Contacts is corrected in your videos too).
- **Search my contacts**: type a name or a handle (at least 2 letters), on any network. The list shows the photo and the accounts of each person (Twitch, YouTube…). One click on **Add** puts them in the video (**6 at most**). With no known photo, the video shows their initials in a coloured circle.
- **Add from a Contacts category**: choose a category (for example the one of your raid targets), add people one by one, or click **"Add the first ones"** to fill the free places. Only the category's Twitch accounts are offered.
- **For a single video**, write a handle, a name, a photo (each optional, but at least one of the first two) in "Add a person for this video only": the person is **never** saved in Contacts. The photo must be a Twitch, YouTube or Bluesky profile photo; otherwise the page tells you.
- **Without Contacts**, everything works: the single-video form remains, and the panel offers to install a module that keeps your contacts. Nothing is installed without your click.
- **The ones of your previous list**: on update, each guest streamer of "My list of guest streamers" becomes a Contacts card (or finds their own), and your models keep the card number. If Contacts is not there, nothing is lost: the list stays, offered as **one-off people**, and the operation redoes itself as soon as Contacts is installed (when the editor opens). A person Contacts cannot take in stays among these one-offs (20 at most).
- The "Take my Twitch Raid partners" button no longer exists: put your partners in a Contacts category and add them from there.

## Your models
Keep your settings (style, format, duration, elements, sentence, guest streamers) **under a name**, for example "Neon evening stream". You can **load** them, **replace** them with the current settings or **delete** them: each time, a confirmation, and the other models do not change. The event itself is not kept in a model: it is always the current one. A model keeps exactly what it stored, including a ticked countdown; only new videos start without it.

## Your AI
Your assistant can prepare a video for you. Four tools, only one of which writes something (reads are granted by default; **the preparation never is**, it is up to you to allow it in the administration, your AI's access):

| Tool | What it does |
|---|---|
| `story_models` | Lists your saved models. Read only. |
| `story_next_streams` | Lists the next streams of your schedule (as before). Read only. |
| `story_events` | Lists the next **events of all sources** (schedule, scheduled YouTube videos and lives, Twitch schedule…), sorted by date and time, in pages of 50 at most, with a search (`q`), a source filter (`source`) and the state of each source. Each event has a stable `id`. Read only. |
| `story_prepare` | Prepares a video (model, settings, optional `watermark`: true for the "Made with Curiosa" mention, false for none; without it, it is your remembered choice, unticked at the start; **event** by its `id` from `story_events`, or stream by its key from `story_next_streams`, or by hand; sentence, duration, guest streamers by their Twitch handle; `style` is one of `neon`, `poster`, `countdown`, `minimal`, `broadcast`, `kinetic`, `cinema`, `arcade`, `collage`, `geometric`) and returns **a link**. A handle that Contacts knows is linked to its card. It only saves a "preparation" kept for 7 days. |

The assistant **does not make the video** (that can only be done in a browser) and **publishes nothing**. You open the link, signed in to your administration: the preview is already filled in, you check, you click "Generate the video" and you save the file. The link only contains a random identifier, no secret. Everything the assistant sends is checked (style, format, duration from 5 to 15 seconds, handles, lengths) and never taken for code: a text stays a text. Without an `elements` list, `elements` defaults to everything except `countdown` (the `countdown` style adds it); an explicit list is respected as given.

## Settings (advanced mode)
- **Colours of the video**: by default those of your site; you can choose others (main colour, second colour, background and text of the "Minimal" style). A text that is too pale is made more readable automatically. Every style derives its palette from these colours and keeps its text readable on its own background.
- **Time zone of the dates** (optional): for example `Europe/Brussels`. It is the one in which the event list writes days and times. Empty, it is the one of your browser. (The site's core does not give it to modules: the setting stays here.)

- **Default "Made with Curiosa" mention**: starting value of the checkbox, unticked as long as you say nothing. As soon as you tick or untick the editor's checkbox, your last choice is remembered and takes precedence.

## Good to know
- **Which browser?** A recent Chrome or Safari (computer or phone) directly makes an MP4 file, which Instagram prefers. With an older browser, the module records the video in real time (it takes longer), and the file may be a WebM: the page tells you, with the advice to try a recent Chrome or Safari. If your browser cannot make video at all, the module explains it **before starting**.
- **While it is being made**, keep the tab open. A 15-second video takes from a few seconds to a minute depending on your device (the "Paper collage" style is the heaviest to draw).
- **Nothing is kept on the site**: the video and the images are never sent or saved. The module only keeps your models (with the card number of each guest streamer), a short list of one-off people without a card, your choice for the mention (a yes/no) and, 7 days at most, your assistant's preparations.
- **The covers** come from the site's covers service (the site owner sets the access once, in the "Game covers" module). With no known cover, the video does very well without it. Profile photos and covers go through the site itself, from the image servers of Twitch, YouTube, Bluesky and the game only; a person's photo always comes from their card in Contacts.
- **Reduce motion**: if your device asks for it, the preview stays on a still image (a "Play" button makes it move); the video itself is always animated.
- **On a phone**, the editor adapts to the screen: the preview is at the top, the settings below, and the style cards stay one per row with their full names.
- **Privacy**: the module collects nothing about your audience and has no public page. Its data is visible to the administration team only.

## For developers
- **Making in the browser**: each style is a function of time, `draw(ctx, t)` (`scenes.mjs` for the first four, `scenes-extra.mjs` for the six added in 2.7.0), run image by image (deterministic, no hazard that cannot be reproduced: grain, paper fibres, stop-motion jitter and film weave are all seeded by constants or by the frame, never by a clock). The encoding uses the browser's H.264 encoder (WebCodecs, 30 images/s, about 8 Mb/s, one key image per second, detected before starting by `VideoEncoder.isConfigSupported`), and an MP4 packer of about 150 lines written for this module (`mp4.mjs`: `ftyp`, `moov` then `mdat`, `avcC`, presentation time lists if the encoder reorders images; it also converts an "Annex B" stream). Fallback: `MediaRecorder` (MP4 if the browser can, otherwise WebM). Only one image is in memory at a time. No third-party library, no CDN.
- **Pure files**: `pure.mjs` (formats, texts, dates, countdown, line breaking, guest layout, colours), `scenes.mjs`, `scenes-extra.mjs`, `mp4.mjs` and `encode.mjs` have no dependency and also run in a blank `vm`; they are served as they are to the page by the `lib/*.mjs` routes.
- **The styles**: one list, `STYLES` in `pure.mjs` (the order is the one of the picker). `createScene` (`scenes.mjs`) lays out the first four itself and hands the six others to `createExtraScene` (`scenes-extra.mjs`) with a "kit" (sizes, content, fonts, small drawing tools); it adds the "Made with Curiosa" mention on top for every style. Adding a style: add its id to `STYLES`, its palette to `extraPalette`, a `styleXxx(K)` function returning `{ build(u, area), place, bg, begin, block, fg }` to `STYLE_IMPL`, and its `ui_style_<id>` / `ui_style_<id>_hint` texts to both locale files and to `KEYS` in `page.mjs`. The shared engine (`xLayout`) stacks the blocks, finds the biggest scale that fits by bisection and returns the boxes the safe-zone test checks.
  - **Craft rules** the six styles share: eased moves (ease-out for entrances, ease-in-out for moves, overshoot only on a stamp), staggered entrances that all end before `restTime` (78 % of the video), then a hold that is perfectly still (`stream-story-scenes` checks that every text is at the same place from `restTime` to the end); no text under 20 px; names shrink before being cut; palettes derive from the site's accent, second accent and background (a one-colour site gets a partner colour by turning the hue) and keep 4.5:1 for text; only system font stacks, per-glyph drawing for tracked capitals. The words the styles write themselves (`À SUIVRE` / `UP NEXT`, `START`) are in `EXTRA_WORDS` for both languages.
  - **Default elements**: `DEFAULT_ELEMENTS` (all but `countdown`) in `pure.mjs` is the starting list of a new video. `STYLE_NEEDS` and `elementsForStyle(style, elements)` say which element a style needs (`countdown` → `countdown`); the editor (`chooseStyle` in `app.mjs`) and `story_prepare` use them. A saved model or preparation keeps exactly the list it stored.
  - **The picker**: one radio card per style, with a small canvas drawn from the real scene at `restTime` (`renderThumbs` in `app.mjs`, lazily, after each rebuild, only on step 3, with the current content and images).
- **Formats**: a single table, `FORMATS` in `pure.mjs`: `{ id, width, height, margins, zones }`. The 4:5 ratio of the "Passport" changes there in one line.
- **The events** (`events.mjs`, `sources.mjs`): two ways, no source is named in the code. (1) The `planning.slot` topic (the schedules) becomes the events of type `calendar`. (2) The **`event.source`** service (version 1, `each: true`, **OPTIONAL need**: `requires[].optional`): Story asks every provider (`ctx.api.services.providers`, 10 at most) for the method `upcoming({ from, to, limit, locale })`, **a separate call per source** (`provider`), each bounded (`timing.sourceMs`, 8 s): a silent source does not hold the others back.
  - **Event contract**: `id` (stable, given and prefixed by the source: letters, digits and `: _ . -`, 100 at most), `title` (80), `start` (UTC, ISO 8601; or `allDay: true` + `day` YYYY-MM-DD, anchored at midnight of the time zone), `timezone` (IANA, or empty), `end?`, `type` (`stream` · `live` · `premiere` · `video` · `event` · `other`), `url?` (https), `image?` (https), `games?` (3 at most). Answer: `{ ok: true, source: { kind, label }, state: "ok" | "setup" | "down", message?, note?, events }` (`message`: what to set up, `note`: what to know; 240 characters). Everything is checked again by Story (`cleanEvent`, `readAnswer`): an unreadable event is dropped alone, an unreadable answer is an outage.
  - **Aggregation** (`aggregate`): upcoming or started less than 3 h ago, one per real event (same title or same link, start within 15 minutes; the schedule takes precedence, then Twitch, then YouTube; the other sources go in `alsoIn` and `ids`), sorted on the UTC instant (the time zone and daylight saving change nothing), 100 events per source and **200 in total**. Pagination by opaque cursor (the last element seen), 50 at most per page.
  - **Memory**: the list is kept 45 s per instance ("Try again" bypasses it after 5 s), the last good list of a silent source 15 min; no background task, two simultaneous requests share the same work; the whole list answers within 12 s at most.
  - **Identifier**: `calendar:<hash of the slot id and its start>` for the schedules (the old key of `story_next_streams` stays valid: `legacyKey`), the source's own otherwise. The request keeps `eventId` and `eventSource` (never the models).
- **Links with other modules**: it **consumes** the `planning.slot` topic (provided by any schedule; time-bounded read, `from` / `to` and a cap of 100). The `stream.partner` topic is **no longer read**. It `requires` the **`contact.book` service in version 2, OPTIONAL** (`optional: true`, `minCore` 0.2.1): `search({ q, network?, kind: "person" })` then `resolve` (photo) for the form, `categories` and `accounts` (pages of 20) for a category, `upsertByAccount` and `setAccountProfile` for the migration. Each call is bounded (`timing.bookMs`, 5 s); without a provider, the panel returns a `suggestInstall` block (service `contact.book`, `min` 2, never a module name). It **offers** `privacy.subject` (`each: true`): `export`, `erase`, `remap` on the references kept in the models and the preparations. It also `requires` **`game.cover`, OPTIONAL** ("Game covers" module: `status` then `cover({ title })`, one bounded call per game); failing that, a fallback to the core's old interface `ctx.api.rawg` (permission `rawg`, to be removed in 0.3.0).
- **A guest streamer in a video** (`pure.mjs`, `cleanMember`): `{ id }` (card reference, opaque identifier ≤ 80 characters, nothing else is kept) or `{ login, name, image }` (entered for this video only, free handle OR name). 6 at most. A list of Twitch handles (version 1 model) is read as one-off entries.
- **No conversion any more (2.9.0)**: the 2.0.0 migration of the version 1 co-streamer list (`story_costreamers`) into Contacts cards was removed. `dataVersion` stays 2 with `dataFloor` 2 (data still in version 1 is set aside; the bridge is the tag `v1.50.0`, see `docs/MIGRATIONS.md`) and `migrations[2]` is an empty marker. A list that was never moved stays in the store, declared in `legacyCollections` and `inertLegacyCollections` (counted on the Upkeep page, deletable from it); it is no longer read, proposed or exported. Models that hold plain handles are still read as one-off entries.
- **Routes** (`/m/<key>/…`): `studio` and `lib/*.mjs` (page and code, no data); `data`, `streams`, `prep`, `people` (search), `members` (re-read cards, 24 at most), `events` (GET: `q`, `source`, `cursor`, `limit` ≤ 50, `id`, `key`, `fresh`) and `cover` (GET: the cover of a single game) for the event list, `categories` and `category` (GET), `act` (POST: `adhoc.check`, `adhoc.remove`, `model.save`, `model.remove`, `prefs.watermark`) and `img` (GET) require the `X-Story-Token` header. This signed token (HMAC, 12 h) is given by the administration panel, in the `#` part of the frame's address. Page security policy: `default-src 'none'; script-src 'self'; connect-src 'self'; frame-ancestors 'self'`.
- **Image relay** (`images.mjs`): https, hosts `static-cdn.jtvnw.net`, `media.rawg.io` and, for Contacts photos, `yt3.ggpht.com`, `yt3.googleusercontent.com` and `cdn.bsky.app` only, a known image path for each (a manual photo on another host, or a signed address like Instagram's, is not relayed: initials), no port, no parameters, no credentials; no redirect followed; 3 MB at most; type read from the first bytes; bounded delay; 10-minute cache; 4 downloads at most at a time.
- **Bounded waits**: `timing` in `sources.mjs` (schedule 6 s, an event source 8 s, the whole list 12 s, covers 5 s, Contacts 5 s per call, whole route 20 s) and `images.mjs`; "black hole" tests in `tests/hang-stream-story.test.mjs`.
- **The "Curiosa" wordmark font**: `curiosa-wordmark.woff2` (exact copy of the core's file, `src/components/brand/`: Fraunces italic, subset of the letters of "Curiosa" only, about 1.7 KB) and its licence `curiosa-wordmark.OFL.txt` (SIL Open Font License 1.1, © The Fraunces Project Authors). Served by the module's `wordmark` route, loaded by `FontFace` (3 s delay; fallback Georgia italic 600) BEFORE building the scene, because the canvas otherwise silently takes a fallback font. No CDN, no outside call.
- **The mention** (`scenes.mjs`, option `watermark: true` of `createScene`, `WATERMARK_TEXT` in `pure.mjs`, not translated): two segments on the same baseline: "Made with" at 30 px, weight 600, the video's font, then "Curiosa" in Fraunces italic 600 at 1.15 times (34.5 px), the whole centred (`measureText`), in the centre, bottom of the safe area (the content loses 82 px of usable height when it is there); white or near-black text depending on the worst contrast against the style's background (start, end and average of the gradient), pill of the background colour at 60 % (darkened or lightened just enough if neither exceeds 4.5:1). Drawn last at each `draw`, independent of `t` (about ten more drawing calls per image). `config.watermark` is a boolean (false by default; only `true` counts), kept in the preparations, never in the models. The remembered choice is in `story_prefs` (the core does not let a module write its settings); the `watermark` setting = starting value; `act` route: `prefs.watermark`. `story_prepare`: optional boolean `watermark`, and `prepared.watermark` in the answer.
- **Storage**: `story_models` and `story_adhoc` (one document each), `story_prefs` (the mention choice), `story_preps` (7 days, 30 at most), `story_key` (signing key); `story_costreamers` (the version 1 list) is a leftover that nothing reads.
